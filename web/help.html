webgym help

webgym is a reinforcement learning playground that runs entirely in your browser: the physics (box2d), the environments and the learning algorithms are python, running in WebAssembly (emscripten-forge + pyjs). Nothing runs on a server. Pick an environment, press Train and watch the agents learn, live.

The page

Top bar (always in view): the live numbers: the world, is it training, which iteration, the population's mean and best return, and what you watch: the training iteration its policy comes from and the episode's return so far. On the right: </> Code (the python editor), the Tour and this help.

Left panel: on top two rows of icons (hover them for what they do). The first: the button with the barbell icon trains (blue while training; a click stops it, the next one continues; the world you watch keeps going either way); the circling arrows start a new run from scratch; the skip-back icon starts a new episode (or R); ⏸ pauses the world you watch (Space; ▶ continues; training goes on); then two switches (blue: on): the loop is auto reset, the crosshair camera follows. The second: − / + how many policies you watch at once, and the replay speed (slower / faster). See "Watching a policy" below. Then five tabs: World (pick the environment and its settings, e.g. how many balls; what it is about), Training (time budget, the algorithm and its parameters, population, simulation workers), Policy (the network and the checkpoints), Toys (things to throw into the world), Log (state, packages, messages). Middle: the world, the training progress below it (click its title to hide it). The ⓘ next to a control explains it (click it).

In the world: drag the background to pan, the mouse wheel zooms, drag a body to push it around (while watching). On a touch screen one finger is the mouse, two fingers pinch to zoom and pan.

On a phone: upright, the world is on top and the tabs below it; sideways, the world fills the screen and ☰ opens the tabs over it. Tour, Help, About and GitHub are in the ⋯ menu; the code editor opens full screen (✕ closes it).

Training

Train (the button with the barbell icon) starts learning: a whole population of agents in one world (they don't collide with each other), each trying a different policy, in a worker of its own, as fast as it can. The world you see shows the current policy, updated as it learns.

Time budget
Quick (~1 min), medium (~5 min) or long (~20 min): picks the algorithm and settings the environment suggests for that time (★ in the algorithm list). Rough guesses.
Algorithm
CEM, ES and NEAT (numpy): evolutionary; they try many random variations of the policy and keep the best ones (NEAT also grows the network itself). Fast and robust for small problems. PPO, A2C, REINFORCE, SAC, TD3 and DQN (pytorch, loaded in the background the first time): gradient based. DQN uses a few discrete actions (e.g. the lander: nothing / main engine / left / right).
Algorithm parameters
Each has an ⓘ. layers and hidden set the size of the policy network (shown below as "Policy network 8 → 16 → 2"); changing them starts a new run.
Population
How many agents learn at once.
Simulation workers
More workers (each its own python and box2d, on another CPU core) run a share of the population's episodes: CEM and ES train several times faster. Each costs memory; more than your CPU cores don't help.
Train / new run (above the tabs)
The button with the barbell icon (blue: training): a click stops and keeps the run, the next continues it. New run (the circling arrows) starts from scratch. Picking another world starts training by itself.

Watching a policy

When not training, the world shows the current policy in orange and, as ghosts, the best candidates of the last iteration (CEM / ES) or recent policies (PPO / DQN). They all get the same random start, so you can compare them fairly; each episode a new one. (While training, the page uses a second worker for this: you watch the latest policy while the training goes on.)

The ghosts are drawn behind the current policy. The ground is a ruler with the metres numbered, so you see how far a walker got; the cart poles mark their hinges with small discs; the water cannon's water is drawn as a liquid (metaballs: a small shader). Sensors are not drawn.

Auto reset (the loop above the tabs)
A new episode starts when all agents are done. Off: the episode goes on after a fall; R starts a new one.
Show policies (− / + above the tabs)
How many policies you watch at once (1: only the orange one).
Replay speed (slower / faster above the tabs)
Slow motion or fast forward.
Camera follows (the crosshair above the tabs)
The environment controls the camera: it says which point the view follows (the walkers' hull, the water cannon's cart; while training the leading agent). Off: a fixed view you pan and zoom yourself. Environments with a fixed scene (CartPole, the lander) don't move the camera, so the option is greyed out there. In your own env: return the point from camera_target(i).
Reset episode (above the tabs, or R)
A new episode now.

Progress, network, checkpoints

Training progress (under the world): per iteration the mean and the best return of the population (higher is better); PPO / DQN also show their losses next to it.

Network (Policy tab): the watched policy's neural network, live. Left the inputs (what the agent sees, with their values), right the actions. Neurons are colored by activation (orange positive, blue negative), connections by their weight.

Checkpoints (Policy tab, below the network): stored in your browser, per environment (with its settings) + algorithm. The autosave is updated while training (a reload continues where you were); give a name and press Save to keep one. Restore loads it, Delete removes it. Below them the pretrained runs that come with the page, one per algorithm, the best first, named after their algorithm (ppo, cem, neat, …): Restore switches to that algorithm and its network, and Train continues it. A link can open any of them by name, yours too: ?env=Walker&checkpoint=ppo (see URL options).

Log tab: the detailed state, what was loaded (e.g. pytorch), messages / errors.

Editing the python code

</> Code opens the source of the current environment or algorithm. Edit it and press Apply (Ctrl/Cmd+Enter): the python module is reloaded in the browser. An edited environment starts a new run, an edited algorithm takes over the training. Open… loads a .py file from your machine, Save… writes one. Your edits are kept in this browser (also after a reload); Revert brings the original back.

Try: in the lander, change gravity or PAD (the width of the landing pad), or the reward in reward. Or the looks: a body's color=, or a draw(debug_draw) method that adds lines, discs or water to the picture (the blob's springs, the cart pole's hinge).

Having fun with agents

A trained agent has only ever seen its own episodes. What does it do when you mess with it? Try this with the walker:

  1. World tab: pick Walker. Policy tab: Restore a pretrained run (they come with the page), or press Train and wait a few minutes, then Stop.
  2. Open the Toys tab: it shows only the policy (no ghosts) and switches Auto reset off, so the episode goes on after a fall instead of starting over.
  3. Let it walk.
  1. Grab its hull with the mouse, lift it and turn it over (the camera follows it).
  1. Let go and watch how it reacts: it never trained for this. Sometimes it catches itself, sometimes it kicks on its back.

The Toys tab

Even more mischief: in the Toys tab you choose what a click into the world does: drop a box, a ball or a plank, set off an explosion, or put a ramp, a step or a seesaw on the ground in front of the agent. Or change the world's gravity (its own fold), sideways (a wind) and up-down (the walker on the moon! or everything falling up), or let it rain. The settings of each (size, weight, rain rate, ...) are in its fold. A click on a body still grabs it. Opening the tab switches auto reset off and shows one policy, without a score (no time limit applies); R (reset) clears the world again. Leaving the tab starts a new episode in the world as it was, with auto reset on again.

The Blob and the Drone

The Blob, a soft ring of balls on springs, learns within a minute to roll and hop to the right by stretching and squeezing its five springs across. Grab a ball and pull: it is soft.

The Drone learns to fly through a course of gates by tilting itself with its two rotors (PPO learns it in a minute or two). Push it off course with the mouse and watch it find its way back.

Tips

  • CartPole and the Blob learn in seconds, LunarLander and the Drone (PPO) in a minute or so, the walkers take longer. MultiCartPole with 4 or 5 poles is hard: try the pretrained runs.
  • Links can open the page in a given state, e.g. watching a pretrained ragdoll: ?env=Ragdoll&checkpoint=ppo. All options: URL options.
  • The first visit downloads python and the packages; later visits come from the browser cache and start in about a second.

URL options

Add them to the page's address after a ?, joined with &, e.g. ?env=Walker&checkpoint=ppo. The page keeps env and algo in the address up to date, so a copied link opens the same world.

env
The world: CartPole, MultiCartPole, LunarLander, Walker, Crawler, Ragdoll, WaterCannon, Blob, Drone. Unknown: the default (CartPole).
a world's settings
As in its World tab: poles=2 … 5 (MultiCartPole), balls=1 or 2 (WaterCannon), e.g. ?env=MultiCartPole&poles=4.
algo
The algorithm: cem, es, neat (numpy); ppo_torch, a2c_torch, reinforce_torch, sac_torch, td3_torch, dqn_torch (pytorch). Without it: the world's suggestion for the time budget.
checkpoint
Watch a checkpoint of the world right away, by its name as listed in the Policy tab (instead of continuing the autosave, and without starting to train). All kinds alike: a pretrained one, named after its algorithm (ppo, cem, es, neat, sac, dqn, …), one you saved in this browser (the newest of that name; it comes first if it has a pretrained one's name), or autosave. E.g. ?env=Ragdoll&checkpoint=ppo, ?env=CartPole&checkpoint=my%20best%20run. Not found: the autosave as usual.
train=0
Don't start training when the page opens (it does by default, continuing your autosave if there is one).
population
How many agents learn at once (as the slider in the Training tab), up to 256.
seed
The random seed of training and of the episodes you watch (a number), for reproducible runs.
helpers
How many simulation workers (as the slider in the Training tab; 0: none). Without it: what you chose last, else about half your CPU cores.
arch
wasm64 or wasm32: which build of python to run. Without it: wasm64 where the browser supports it (Chrome, Firefox), else wasm32 (Safari).
dpr
Drawing pixels per screen pixel, e.g. dpr=0.5 for a faster, blurrier world on a slow device. Without it: the screen's, but at most about a million pixels.
tour=0
Skip the welcome card on a first visit.
welcome=1
Always show the welcome card (with the tours), also after the first visit.

About

Python in the browser is no longer a toy. emscripten-forge is a conda channel of packages compiled to WebAssembly: python, numpy, box2d (pyb2d3), pytorch and many more, installed the way you know from conda / pixi. pyjs runs them in the browser (here: in Web Workers, drawing with WebGL on an offscreen canvas) and bridges python and JavaScript.

Why? Above all for education. Python is the language people learn programming, science and machine learning with, and the hardest part is often not the idea but the setup: installing python and its packages on every machine of a class, a workshop or a lecture hall. In the browser there is nothing to install. A link is enough, and it works the same on every laptop, tablet or phone, with no server doing the computing for thousands of students.

And it is interactive: here the reinforcement learning is not a plot at the end of a script, you see the agents learn, live; the python is right there, you can read it, change it, and see at once what happens. Along the way webgym shows what runs in a browser today: a physics engine, a deep learning framework and the code that trains it, on your own machine.

The code: github.com/DerThorsten/webgym.
