webgym help

webgym is a reinforcement learning playground that runs entirely in your browser: the physics (box2d), the environments and the learning algorithms are python, running in WebAssembly (emscripten-forge + pyjs). Nothing runs on a server. Pick an environment, press Train and watch the agents learn, live.

The page

The top bar

Always in view. On the left the live numbers:

  • the world (here the Walker)
  • whether it is training, and which iteration
  • the population's mean and best return
  • what you watch: the training iteration its policy comes from, and the episode's return so far

On the right: opens the python editor (see Editing the python code), then the Tour, this Help, About and GitHub.

The buttons

On top of the left panel, a row of icons (hover one for what it does):

  • Train: starts learning. Blue while training; a click stops it, the next one continues.
  • New run: forget what was learned, start from scratch.
  • New episode (or R): the watched agent starts over.
  • Pause (or Space): freezes the world you watch; ▶ continues. Training goes on.
  • Auto reset (a switch, blue: on): a new episode starts when one ends.
  • Camera follows (a switch): the view follows the agent.
  • Replay speed: slow motion or fast forward.

More on them in Watching a policy.

The tabs

  • World: pick the environment and its settings (e.g. how many balls), and read what it is about.
  • Training: time budget, algorithm and its parameters, population, simulation workers.
  • Policy: the network and the checkpoints.
  • Toys: things to throw into the world.
  • Log: state, loaded packages, messages.

The next to a control explains it (click it).

The world

  • In the middle: the world, and below it the training progress (: click the title to hide it).
  • Drag the background to pan, the mouse wheel zooms.
  • Drag a body to push it around.
  • On a touch screen: one finger is the mouse, two fingers pinch to zoom and pan.

On a phone

  • Upright: the world on top, the tabs below it.
  • Sideways: the world fills the screen, ☰ opens the tabs over it.
  • Tour, Help, About and GitHub are in the ⋯ menu.
  • The code editor opens full screen (✕ closes it).

Training

Train starts learning:

  • a whole population of agents in one world (they don't collide with each other), each trying a different policy;
  • in a worker of its own, as fast as it can;
  • the world you see shows the current policy, updated as it learns.

Picking another world starts training by itself.

The Training tab

Time budget
Quick (~1 min), medium (~5 min) or long (~20 min). Picks the algorithm and settings the environment suggests for that time (★ in the algorithm list). Rough guesses.
Algorithm

Evolutionary (numpy): they try many variations of the policy, each for a whole episode, and keep what scored best. Simple, robust, fast for small problems.

  • CEM (cross-entropy method): samples a population of random policies around a mean, then moves the mean to the best few of them.
  • ES (evolution strategies): nudges the policy in random directions and steps towards the directions that scored better, weighted by how much better.
  • NEAT (neuro-evolution of augmenting topologies): evolves the network's shape too. It starts tiny and adds neurons and connections over the generations.

Gradient based (pytorch, loaded in the background the first time): they learn from every step, which works better for harder problems.

  • REINFORCE: the classic policy gradient. Makes the actions of good episodes more likely, those of bad ones less.
  • A2C (advantage actor-critic): REINFORCE plus a critic that estimates how good a state is. Actions are judged against that estimate, which steadies the learning.
  • PPO (proximal policy optimization): actor-critic with small, careful update steps, so one bad batch can't ruin the policy. A good default.
  • SAC (soft actor-critic): learns from a memory of past steps (replay buffer) and rewards exploring. Data-efficient for continuous actions.
  • TD3 (twin delayed DDPG): also learns from a replay buffer, with a deterministic policy and two critics against overestimating.
  • DQN (deep Q-network): learns the value of each action from a replay buffer and picks the best one. It works only with a few discrete actions (e.g. the lander: nothing / main engine / left / right).

Your own:

  • My algorithm (numpy): a hill climber, about the simplest thing that learns. It tries small random changes of the best policy so far and keeps the one that scored best. A starting point for your own algorithm, see Your own environment and algorithm.
Algorithm parameters
Each has an . layers and hidden set the size of the policy network (shown as "Policy network 8 → 16 → 2"); changing them starts a new run.
Population
How many agents learn at once.
Simulation workers
  • More workers (each its own python and box2d, on another CPU core) run a share of the population's episodes.
  • CEM and ES train several times faster with them.
  • Each costs memory; more than your CPU cores don't help.

Stop and continue with (blue: training; a stopped run is kept), start from scratch with .

Watching a policy

  • The world shows one agent acting with the current policy, each episode from a new random start.
  • While training, the page watches in a second worker: the latest policy the trainer sent takes over with the next episode.
  • The ground is a ruler with numbered metres: you see how far an agent got.
  • A disc marks each joint; the water cannon's water is drawn as a liquid (metaballs: a small shader). Sensors are not drawn.
Auto reset
A new episode starts when the agent's episode ends. Off: the episode goes on after a fall; R starts a new one.
Camera follows
  • The environment says which point the view follows (the walkers' hull, the water cannon's cart).
  • Off: a fixed view you pan and zoom yourself.
  • Worlds with a fixed scene (CartPole, the lander) never move the camera: the switch is greyed out there.
  • In your own env: return the point from camera_target(i).
Replay speed
Slow motion or fast forward.
New episode (or R)
A new episode now.
Pause (or Space)
Freezes the world you watch; training goes on.

Progress, network, checkpoints

Training progress (under the world, ), per iteration:

  • the population's mean and best return (higher is better);
  • PPO / DQN also show their losses next to it.

Network (Policy tab): the watched policy's neural network, live.

  • Left the inputs (what the agent sees, with their values), right the actions.
  • Neurons are colored by activation: orange positive, blue negative.
  • Connections are colored by their weight.

Checkpoints (Policy tab, below the network), stored in your browser per environment (with its settings) and algorithm:

  • autosave: updated while training, so a reload continues where you were.
  • Your own: give a name and press Save. Restore loads one, Delete removes it.
  • Pretrained runs come with the page, one per algorithm, named after it (ppo, cem, neat, …). Restore switches to that algorithm and its network; Train continues it.
  • A link opens any of them by name, yours too: ?env=Walker&checkpoint=ppo (see URL options).

Log tab: the detailed state, what was loaded (e.g. pytorch), messages / errors.

Editing the python code

(top bar) opens the source of the current environment or algorithm (choose which in the select above the editor).

  • (or Ctrl/Cmd+Enter): reloads the python module in the browser. An edited environment starts a new run, an edited algorithm takes over the training.
  • loads a .py file from your machine, writes one.
  • brings the original back.
  • Your edits are kept in this browser, also after a reload.

Things to try:

  • In the lander: change gravity or PAD (the width of the landing pad), or the reward in reward.
  • The looks: a body's color=.
  • A draw(debug_draw) method that adds lines, discs or water to the picture (as the blob's springs, the cart pole's hinge).

Your own environment and algorithm

Two entries are there just for you to change. They are starting points for your own experiments and fun ideas:

  • MyEnvironment (last in the World tab's list): a ball learns to roll to a target. It is the smallest world, and it shows every part an environment needs:
    • build_scenery: the static world (the ground);
    • build_agent: one agent (a ball);
    • randomize: the start of an episode (a new target);
    • act: what the actions do (push the ball);
    • observe: what the agent sees (where the target is, how fast it rolls);
    • reward: what counts as good (being close to the target);
    • draw: extra looks (the target disc).
  • My algorithm (last in the Training tab's list): a hill climber in about 30 lines. Each iteration it runs one episode with all agents, each with a small random change of the best policy so far, and keeps the one that scored best.

How to make them yours:

  1. Pick MyEnvironment (World tab) or My algorithm (Training tab).
  2. Open and choose environment or algorithm above the editor.
  3. Change something and press .
  4. Press and watch what happens.

Ideas to start with:

  • A target in the air: the ball has to jump.
  • A wall or a slope in the way.
  • A second ball, or a box on top of the ball.
  • In the algorithm: keep the best few and average them, or shrink the changes over time.

Your edits stay in this browser. writes the file to your machine. The other worlds and algorithms show more (joints, motors, sensors, pytorch); their code is open the same way.

Having fun with agents

A trained agent has only ever seen its own episodes. What does it do when you mess with it? Try this with the walker:

  1. World tab: pick Walker. Policy tab: Restore a pretrained run (they come with the page), or press and wait a few minutes, then stop it.
  2. Open the Toys tab: it switches auto reset off, so the episode goes on after a fall instead of starting over.
  3. Let it walk.
  1. Grab its hull with the mouse, lift it and turn it over (the camera follows it).
  1. Let go and watch how it reacts: it never trained for this. Sometimes it catches itself, sometimes it kicks on its back.

The Toys tab

Even more mischief. In the Toys tab you choose what a click into the world does, with buttons like these:

  • Things to drop: a box, a ball or a plank.
  • Explosion: blows everything nearby away.
  • Ramps and steps: a ramp, a step or a seesaw on the ground in front of the agent.
  • Gravity: sideways (a wind) and up-down (the walker on the moon! or everything falling up); an arrow in the world shows it while its fold is open.
  • Weather: let it rain.

Good to know:

  • The settings of each (size, weight, rain rate, …) are in its fold.
  • A click on a body still grabs it; a click on the active tool puts it away.
  • Opening the tab switches auto reset off: no score, no time limit.
  • R clears the world again.
  • Leaving the tab starts a new episode in the world as it was, auto reset on.

The Blob and the Drone

The Blob, a soft ring of balls on springs, learns within a minute to roll and hop to the right by stretching and squeezing its five springs across. Grab a ball and pull: it is soft.

The Drone learns to fly through a course of gates by tilting itself with its two rotors (PPO learns it in a minute or two). Push it off course with the mouse and watch it find its way back.

Tips

  • CartPole and the Blob learn in seconds, LunarLander and the Drone (PPO) in a minute or so, the walkers take longer. MultiCartPole with 4 or 5 poles is hard: try the pretrained runs.
  • Links can open the page in a given state, e.g. watching a pretrained ragdoll: ?env=Ragdoll&checkpoint=ppo. All options: URL options.
  • The first visit downloads python and the packages; later visits come from the browser cache and start in about a second.

URL options

Add them to the page's address after a ?, joined with &, e.g. ?env=Walker&checkpoint=ppo. The page keeps env and algo in the address up to date, so a copied link opens the same world.

env
The world: CartPole, MultiCartPole, LunarLander, Walker, Crawler, Ragdoll, WaterCannon, Blob, Drone, MyEnvironment. Unknown: the default (CartPole).
a world's settings
As in its World tab: poles=2 … 5 (MultiCartPole), balls=1 or 2 (WaterCannon), e.g. ?env=MultiCartPole&poles=4.
algo
The algorithm: cem, es, neat (numpy); ppo_torch, a2c_torch, reinforce_torch, sac_torch, td3_torch, dqn_torch (pytorch); my_algorithm. Without it: the world's suggestion for the time budget.
checkpoint
Watch a checkpoint of the world right away, by its name as listed in the Policy tab (instead of continuing the autosave, and without starting to train). All kinds alike: a pretrained one, named after its algorithm (ppo, cem, es, neat, sac, dqn, …), one you saved in this browser (the newest of that name; it comes first if it has a pretrained one's name), or autosave. E.g. ?env=Ragdoll&checkpoint=ppo, ?env=CartPole&checkpoint=my%20best%20run. Not found: the autosave as usual.
train=0
Don't start training when the page opens (it does by default, continuing your autosave if there is one).
population
How many agents learn at once (as the slider in the Training tab), up to 256.
seed
The random seed of training and of the episodes you watch (a number), for reproducible runs.
helpers
How many simulation workers (as the slider in the Training tab; 0: none). Without it: what you chose last, else about half your CPU cores.
arch
wasm64 or wasm32: which build of python to run. Without it: wasm64 where the browser supports it (Chrome, Firefox), else wasm32 (Safari).
dpr
Drawing pixels per screen pixel, e.g. dpr=0.5 for a faster, blurrier world on a slow device. Without it: the screen's, but at most about a million pixels.
tour=0
Skip the welcome card on a first visit.
welcome=1
Always show the welcome card (with the tours), also after the first visit.

About

Python in the browser is no longer a toy. emscripten-forge is a conda channel of packages compiled to WebAssembly: python, numpy, box2d (pyb2d3), pytorch and many more, installed the way you know from conda / pixi. pyjs runs them in the browser (here: in Web Workers, drawing with WebGL on an offscreen canvas) and bridges python and JavaScript.

Why? Above all for education. Python is the language people learn programming, science and machine learning with, and the hardest part is often not the idea but the setup: installing python and its packages on every machine of a class, a workshop or a lecture hall. In the browser there is nothing to install. A link is enough, and it works the same on every laptop, tablet or phone, with no server doing the computing for thousands of students.

And it is interactive: here the reinforcement learning is not a plot at the end of a script, you see the agents learn, live; the python is right there, you can read it, change it, and see at once what happens. Along the way webgym shows what runs in a browser today: a physics engine, a deep learning framework and the code that trains it, on your own machine.

The code: github.com/DerThorsten/webgym.
